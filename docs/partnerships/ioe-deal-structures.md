# Deal structures and rates — Institute of Education

**Hold until asked.** Naming a price before they want the product hands them a reason to
decide without meeting you.

## What we know about their side

| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue (year to Aug 2025) | €26.5m, up 15% |
| Operating profit | €10.5m |
| Staff | 238 |
| Students | ~1,000 full-time; ~1,650 including grinds *(sources differ — ask)* |
| Full-time fees | ~€11,000 |
| Owner | Dukes Education (Apr 2023, ~€130m, 93.3%); group revenue €458.8m, 30+ schools |
| Also Irish | Bruce College, Cork |

Two consequences. **They can afford anything being contemplated here** — the numbers below
are immaterial against €10.5m of operating profit, so never negotiate as though price is the
obstacle; the obstacle is risk and staff time. And **PE ownership means process** — expect
legal review, a DPA, and possibly group sign-off from London. Budget months, not weeks.

---

## Set the list price first

**€9.99/month, direct to student.** Publish it. Everything offered to a school is then a
visible discount off a real number rather than a figure invented at the table — which is
worth more in the negotiation than the revenue it earns.

**The framing to use:**

> Dublin grinds run €30–40 an hour. NumScoil is €9.99 a month — a third of one grind, and it
> marks every piece of homework the student does.

Compare to grinds, not to software. Grinds are what her customers already pay for, and the
comparison flatters.

**The honest problem with it:** €9.99/month is ≈€90 over a nine-month school year and ≈€120
annually. **Studyclix is €35–75/year with 250,000 users.** You are pricing at 1.5–3× the
incumbent. That is defensible — they show the marking scheme, you mark the student's work —
but the comparison *will* be made, so have the answer ready and consider a **€79–99 annual
option** to blunt it. Do not quietly drop the monthly price to match; that concedes you are
the same product.

**VAT:** quote €9.99 VAT-inclusive to consumers. Irish VAT on digital services is 23%, and
registration thresholds matter as this scales. Get an accountant's view **before** the price
goes public — changing it afterwards is far more costly than setting it right.

---

## Open with a pilot

Given ~19 non-staff users and no outcome data, the pilot is the realistic ask. Frame it as
prudence on their part, not weakness on yours.

**One term, one 6th Year Higher Level cohort.**

- **€3,000–5,000 fixed**, or
- **free**, in exchange for a named reference and permission to publish anonymised outcome
  data

Prefer the free version if they will commit to the reference and the data. A named
Institute of Education reference and a published outcome study is worth far more than €5,000
— it is the thing that makes every subsequent school conversation winnable, and it fixes the
"what are the results?" gap permanently.

**Insist on:** a named teacher who owns it internally, a minimum number of students, a
defined end date, and an agreed decision point. Pilots without an end date become permanent
free usage.

## Option A — Site licence *(recommended)*

Their brand or co-branded; every student in the year group has access, bundled into existing
fees so students pay nothing extra.

| | |
|---|---|
| Rate | **€25–35 per student per year** |
| At ~1,000 students | €25,000–35,000/year |
| At ~1,650 students | €41,000–58,000/year |
| Setup and branding | €5,000–10,000 one-off |

**The line that closes it:**

> Against fees of €11,000, thirty euro a year is **0.27% of what a student already pays** —
> and it reads as a 70% discount on our list price, because you are bringing us the whole
> year group at once.

**Why recommend this one:** predictable, no revenue-share accounting or reporting, no
dependence on their sales effort, and it covers students who never log in. It becomes a
budget line rather than a commercial project, which is a much smaller internal decision than
Option B.

---

## Option B — Revenue share

Co-branded; they promote it, students subscribe directly at €9.99/month.

| | |
|---|---|
| Their commission | **30–40% of net** |
| Morgan nets | €6–7 per student per month |
| 800 subscribers | ≈€60k gross/year → €36–42k to Morgan |

Higher ceiling, and it lets them earn margin rather than spend budget — which some schools
prefer. But it depends entirely on their marketing effort, and if they do nothing you earn
nothing.

**So if this is the structure, require a minimum annual guarantee.** Without one you have
given a large organisation an option on your product at no cost to them.

They have an asset worth noting here: a large current-student and alumni list, and grinds
students beyond the full-time cohort. If they will actually mail it, Option B can beat
Option A. Judge that on whether they commit to specific promotion, not on enthusiasm in a
meeting.

## Content-only licence — resist

They take the 516 questions into their own Moodle and drop the platform.

This hands over the asset, leaves nothing recurring, and turns a business into a one-off
payment. If pressed, price it at **six figures**, term-limited, non-exclusive, with the
marking engine explicitly excluded — priced to be declined. The marking is the product; the
questions are the inventory.

## Terms to hold

**Ownership.** The software and content are owned and maintained by us; the Institute
licenses them. Put this in writing early and unprompted — it prevents a misunderstanding
becoming a negotiation later. Work commissioned specifically for them can be theirs.

⚠️ **Establish provenance of the question bank before warranting ownership of it.** Any
licence will contain an IP warranty, and you cannot warrant what you cannot evidence. The
`is_copyrighted` flag currently says True for 202 questions and False for 314, and its
meaning is documented in opposite directions in `Question.is_copyrighted`'s help text and in
the `extract-question` skill. Settle the convention, correct the rows, and know which
questions are yours — before signing anything that says they all are. This is the one item
here with genuine legal exposure.

**Exclusivity is never free.** A Dukes-owned school may well ask for Irish exclusivity. If
so, it requires a minimum annual guarantee, a time limit (one year, renewable on performance),
and a carve-out for existing schools and direct student subscriptions. Never grant it as
goodwill — it is the single most expensive thing you could give away, and it is the thing
most likely to be asked for casually.

**Term.** One year initial, auto-renewing, 90 days' notice. Long enough to justify building
for them, short enough that they will sign it.

**Data protection.** These are minors, and the counterparty is PE-owned with real lawyers.
Expect a DPA and a controller/processor discussion. Have answers ready on where data is
hosted, retention, and **OpenAI as a sub-processor** — that last one will be asked, so name
it before they find it. Get a solicitor to review anything they send; do not sign a DPA
unadvised.

**Payment.** Annual in advance, or termly. Not in arrears — you would be financing a company
with €10.5m of operating profit.

**Support.** Define it before they define it for you: response times, term-time cover, and
who to contact. Left vague, "support" expands to mean whatever they need at exam time.

## Expansion — mention once

Bruce College (Cork) is Dukes-owned, and the group runs 30+ schools with €458.8m of revenue.
Ordinary Level and further subjects are roadmap.

Say it once as upside. **Do not price it in and do not let it be priced in** — a group-wide
rate agreed off the back of a single-school pilot is how a good deal becomes a bad one. If
they raise a group deal themselves, that is a different and much better negotiation, and it
should carry a group-scale minimum.

---

## The walk-away

Know it before going in. Below roughly **€15–20k/year for a site licence**, or a revenue
share with no minimum guarantee, this costs more in support and account management than it
returns — and it prices the next school. A first customer at a bad rate sets a ceiling that
is very hard to lift later.

Better outcomes than a cheap deal: a paid pilot, or a free pilot with a named reference and
a published outcome study.
